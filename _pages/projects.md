    <div class="title">Constraint Heterogeneity in Dynamic Choice Models</div>
    <div class="author">Doctoral research · Hiroshima University · supervised by Prof. Makoto Chikaraishi</div>
    <div class="periodical">
      A day is assembled out of what is possible, not only out of what is wanted, and what is
      possible differs between people. A model that cannot carry that difference reads constraint
      as preference, and the error propagates into accessibility and welfare; models that can
      carry it exactly have paid one value function per person. This work asks what per-person
      feasibility actually costs — classifying constraints by what they read, and so by how many
      distinct computations a population genuinely requires.

    <div class="title">A Scalable Computational Framework for Activity-Based Dynamic Discrete Choice Models</div>
    <div class="author">Master's thesis · Hiroshima University · defended July 2026</div>
    <div class="periodical">
      Exact estimation of activity-based DDCMs at city scale. Because the value of a day is a
      log-sum over every feasible day, nothing may be dropped for being unlikely — only for being
      impossible. A forward search under the space-time prism keeps just the reachable states,
      cutting the theoretical state space by more than ninety-nine percent without approximation;
      the pruned graph is acyclic, so a whole time layer solves at once; and one solve is shared
      across everyone facing the same constraints. Estimated on the Higashi-Hiroshima travel diary.
    </div>
